IBISWorld

My IBISWorld

IBISWorld - an Australian research company - wanted to restructure their current, old legacy existing client based website into a more modern take. This fresh now web based app appropriately dubbed ‘My IBISWorld’, allows a user to swiftly browse and search for reports within different product offerings, download multiple different report methods, bookmark reports and has shiny, happy interactive charts (plus a whole bunch of other good stuff). Needless to say IBISWorld is no longer gonna party like it’s 1999.

Role

Lead Product Designer

Duration

1.1 years (May 2019 – June 2020)

Responsibilities

Conduct user interviews. Plan and scope requirements. Feedback sessions with multi-country stakeholders. Work with developers on technicality feasibility. Create and maintain IW Design System.

Before the magic

✦ ✦

How can we create a B2B platform that heightens data visualization within digital reports, become fully WCAG complaint for users while bring a fresh modern spin to the IBISWorld platform for all types of users?

usability goals

IBISWorld prioritized WCAG compliance to meet the strict requirements of academic clients. The platform also caters to Gen X and Baby Boomer users who value a clean interface for quickly reading and printing reports for meetings.

business goals

To drive renewals and attract new clients, IBISWorld integrated interactive data visualizations and improved accessibility. These updates ensure reports are easier to explore while maintaining full regulatory compliance.

Predicted outcome

Revamping the core product with a modern, intuitive UI directly supports client retention and acquisition. By streamlining UX functionality, IBISWorld strengthens its market appeal and fosters long-term client relationships.

Studying the charts

✦ ✦

Before diving into the design process, I interviewed a few current IBISWorld clients to get their take on how they would like a new platform to function and look

Keepin’ it old school

Most users are long time clients to IBISWorld that wanted to still have the capabilities to download and print reports to take into meetings

I don’t see the vision

Users felt the data visualizations didn’t align with the information provided. They requested higher-level overviews and interactive states for better data exploration.

Written education

Users claimed to wanting more in-depth analysis reports, however that is not a UX problem but an overall product content/feature problem for report writers.

About My IBISWorld

Brewing up the design process

✦ ✦

I led UX research through interviews with stakeholders and clients to identify core needs and strengthen the brand loyalty. Translated insights into streamlined user flows and wireframes under tight deadlines. Developed the “IDEAL” design system leveraging Material Design, enabling faster dev collaboration across an 8-person team. Prioritized accessibility through WCAG audits and vendor evaluation post-launch.


I Introduced a universal search experience (landing, header, in-report) based on user demand and designed a new report navigation system, allowing seamless chapter and related report access—improving usability and engagement.

Home Page

The home page was designed as an evolving dashboard, initially focusing on quick access to saved, searched, and previous reports. We also built a new navigation system to clearly display product features based on the client’s specific subscription.

Wireframes

Industry Reports

Industry reports are IBISWorld’s bread and butter. I overhauled these detailed search results—covering everything from farming to travel—with interactive High Charts visualizations for easier development and better UX. I also added a side navigation to allow users to search, download, and jump between report chapters seamlessly.

Wireframes

Data Visualizations

Post-launch, I addressed accessibility issues in legacy visualizations by redesigning them with Highcharts. Using tools like the Colorblinding extension, I introduced a colorblind-friendly palette, added patterns and shape markers for clarity, and increased spacing to improve readability for users with visual impairments.

Time to see if the magic holds

✦ ✦

I conducted User Testing with current clients of IBISWorld. I spoke with a range of clients user types via Skype.

You betta bet on bugs

Beta testing revealed numerous code bugs in the product, which proved valuable for identifying issues before the official launch.

Where’s the f*@$ing button?

Users initially struggled to find the report download button, so the icon was repositioned next to the report title, resulting in improved user success with downloads.

Dealer’s Choice

5/6 users loved the ability to customize their preferences through bookmarks, in-app notifications, and the ability to download multiple formats of reports. In all this was something the old product did not offer users.

🎵 Can you paint with all the colors of accessibility 🎵

Feedback from a colorblind user revealed issues with the notification icon visibility and complex data visualizations. In response, version 1.3 of MyIBISWorld improved icon color and redesigned visualizations for better accessibility.
