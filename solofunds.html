Solo Funds

Debit Card Verification

SoLo Funds is a peer-to-peer micro-lending platform where community members lend and borrow from each other. To protect lenders and borrowers on the platform, SoLo introduced a debit card verification flow to reduce fraud and increase trust within the community.

This case study is under an NDA. Specific metrics and strategy details have been omitted, but the design process and outcomes are shared here.

Role

Lead Product Designer

Duration

6.5 Months
(Apr 2022 – Oct 2022)

Responsibilities

Collaborated with Data Scientists and Product Managers to define requirements, led end-to-end design from research through delivery, conducted usability testing via Maze, and iterated on designs based on findings and stakeholder feedback.

Before the magic

Nearly 34% of SoLo users experience fraud activity on the platform. The team needed to design a debit card verification flow that would protect users while keeping the onboarding experience frictionless and trustworthy.

Usability Goals

Design a seamless, intuitive debit card verification flow that users can complete quickly and with confidence. Minimize drop-off by reducing cognitive load and making each step feel safe and necessary.

Business Goals

Reduce fraud on the SoLo platform, increase lender trust, and improve overall community health. Card verification was seen as a key trust signal that would drive both lender and borrower retention.

Predicted Outcome

A verified debit card flow would reduce fraudulent transactions, increase lender confidence in the platform, and create a healthier peer-to-peer lending ecosystem that benefits both sides of the marketplace.

Studying the charts

Before diving into the design process, I sent out a Maze survey to current SoLo users to understand their awareness of fraud, their feelings about connecting financial information, and what other fintech apps they use. The data was eye-opening.

27%

of users reported losing their phone or having it stolen

89%

felt that bank connection gives them a sense of safety on the platform

93%

were unaware that other fintech apps offered fraud prevention features

Brewing up the design process

Armed with research and a clear problem to solve, I leveraged the survey findings to shape the design direction. Working closely with Data Scientists and the Product Manager, I mapped out the verification states, edge cases, and error scenarios before touching a single frame.

The biggest challenge was balancing security requirements with user trust—nobody wants to feel interrogated when they’re just trying to borrow money for groceries. I focused on progressive disclosure, clear microcopy, and reassuring UI patterns to keep the experience feeling supportive rather than suspicious.

New users — version 1

The first version of the debit card verification flow focused on getting users verified as quickly as possible. Wireframes were kept minimal, prioritizing speed over education. The assumption was that users would trust the process if it was fast.

New users — version 2

After usability testing revealed users felt rushed and confused about why they needed to verify, I redesigned the flow with more context and reassurance at each step. Version 2 introduced educational moments and clearer progress indicators to keep users oriented and confident.

Time to see if the magic holds

I ran an unmoderated Maze usability test with 11 participants to validate the Version 2 flow before final handoff. The results gave us clear direction on what was working and what still needed refinement.

Flow too funky

Several participants got confused between the micro-deposit verification step and the instant card verification option. The choice architecture needed clearer hierarchy—users wanted to know which path was faster before committing.

Safety first

Participants responded positively to the trust-building copy and iconography. The messaging around “your information is protected” significantly reduced hesitation. Users felt safe handing over card details when they understood the why behind it.

The finished potion

After two rounds of iteration and testing, the debit card verification flow launched and delivered measurable results. The final design balanced security with empathy, making verification feel like a feature rather than a barrier.

Success Rate

88% of users successfully connected their debit card on the first attempt in post-launch monitoring—a strong signal that the flow was intuitive and trustworthy.

Drop-off

12% of users dropped off during the verification flow. Post-launch, a V2 error handling pass addressed the most common failure states—expired cards, unsupported banks—further reducing abandonment.

What I learned

Security UX is trust UX. The moment users understand why they’re being asked for something sensitive, their willingness to comply shoots up. Investing in the right copy and iconography at the right moments is just as important as the interaction design itself.
